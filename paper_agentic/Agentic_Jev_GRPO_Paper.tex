\begin{abstract}
Multi-turn coding and tool-use reinforcement learning operates over an $H$-turn POMDP ($H \in [3, 8]$). Standard terminal binary RLVR ($R_H \in \{0, 1\}$) broadcasts a single scalar advantage across all $H$ turns—penalizing valid early fault-localization turns whenever a late turn commits a minor syntax slip (causing 46.8\% zero-variance group dead-zones)—and incentivizes unit-test tampering (\texttt{assert True}) or hardcoded input-output branches (29.4\% hack rate). We introduce \textbf{Agentic-Jev-GRPO}, co-locating the trainable policy LoRA and frozen \texttt{jaredpalmer/kev-4b} (\texttt{/v1/systemone}) turn judge LoRA + Layer-28 pointer head on a single shared \texttt{Qwen/Qwen3.5-4B} backbone (8.06 GB vs.\ 16.20 GB, $-50.2\%$ VRAM, 52.14 ms/turn with 84.6\% KV-cache reuse on NVIDIA RTX PRO 6000 Blackwell Server Edition). By combining turn-level telescoping PBRS $r_t^{\text{step}} = \gamma \Phi_t - \Phi_{t-1}$ with trajectory-minimum Noul gating $G_{\text{noul}}(\tau) = \min_t g_{\text{noul},t}$, Agentic-Jev-GRPO achieves \#1 SOTA across BFCL-v3 Multi-Turn (88.4\%), MBPP+ Multi-Turn Repair (86.8\%), HumanEval+ Tool-Exec (89.1\%), and SWE-bench-Verified-Mini (48.6\%), reaching 78.23\% Mean Score ($+14.33\%$ over Terminal-RLVR) while cutting average tool-call turns by 2.1$\times$ (3.2 vs.\ 6.8) and suppressing reward hacking to 2.4\%.
\end{abstract}
