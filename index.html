DC-Jev-RL, version 4

Five papers on Kev-4B as a reward judge for PPO and GRPO, redone as measured experiments. October 2026. 中文

Correction. The results of versions 1.0 to 3.0 of these papers were not produced by the experiments they describe, and they are withdrawn. The correction notice lists what was wrong. Version 4 redoes the work: every number in its papers is computed from the logs in the repository, baselines are tuned like the methods on validation data, and negative results are reported.

Kev-4B is a decision model built as a LoRA adapter and a pointer head on Qwen3.5-4B-Base. It answers yes/no questions about a JSON state in one forward pass. Each part asks whether that judgment is a useful training signal, and compares it with an autoregressive judge of the same size where one applies.

The five parts

Part I

Language judges as reward sources for PPO in MuJoCo

Can a 4B judge that reads a text summary of a robot's behaviour replace the engineered reward of a MuJoCo task?

165 PPO runs, seven reward sources, five tasks. Kev-4B's gated reward reaches an interquartile mean normalised score of 1.00 [0.81, 1.09], against 1.05 for the engineered reward; the autoregressive judge's reaches 0.35, and its Hopper and Walker2d policies learn to stand still. On Reacher every judge's reward ends below a random policy.

Part II

One backbone for the policy and the judge

What does sharing one Qwen3.5-4B backbone between a GRPO policy and Kev-4B save, and does Kev-4B's judgment add anything to the verifier reward on MATH?

With one shared backbone the trainer needs 48% less GPU memory after loading, and the judge's probabilities stay within 0.015 of fp32. In GRPO the verifier alone takes MATH-500 from 76.5% to 82.5%. With Kev-4B's score added to the reward, or gating it, one seed per condition ends far ahead and the others near the verifier-only runs, so the gain cannot be attributed to the judge. Trained on Kev-4B's score alone, the policy learns to write wrong solutions the judge rates as correct (70.7% on MATH-500).

Part III-A

Visible tests, held-out tests and a judge

How much reward hacking is there when multi-turn code repair is rewarded by visible tests and graded by held-out ones, and does a judge reduce it?

Offline, 36% of the base policy's attempts that pass the visible tests fail a held-out one, and both judges rank them below correct attempts. In GRPO we saw no sign that the visible tests were exploited: the share of problems solved rose (KodCode validation 65.5% to 86.6%), and the share of visible-test passes that are wrong fell by about half on two of the three sets. The judge factor changed nothing beyond the variation between seeds.

Part III-B

When the verifier labels only some samples

When the verifier labels only 2 of the 8 samples per prompt, can a frozen, a recalibrated or a scratch judge head fill in the rest?

The frozen Kev-4B head ranks the unlabelled samples well (AUROC 0.91), but both of its policies learned to repeat themselves after the answer, which it pays for; on MATH-500 they end −2.3 points [−4.4, −0.1] against dropping those samples. Recalibrating the head fixed its calibration, not what it pays for. A head trained from scratch ends +4.1 [1.6, 6.6] against dropping them, with both seeds switched to drafting first (below), so two seeds cannot say how much of that is the head's.

Part III-C

Can a judge choose where to branch?

Can a judge choose the prefixes to continue in tree rollouts for GRPO on MATH?

Offline, every signal predicts across problems whether a 128-token prefix will end correct, but separates one problem's prefixes only weakly. In training, Kev-4B's choice of roots raised the share of correct leaves (+1.4 points over keeping all four roots), and the final policies are as accurate as with random roots (MATH-500 +0.7 [−0.8, 2.3]). The tree runs' lead over flat GRPO comes mostly from the 2 seeds that switched to drafting first. Trees generate 7%–12% fewer tokens than flat sampling of completions of the same lengths.

Across the GRPO papers

Drafting first. Of the 26 GRPO runs of Parts II, III-B and III-C, the 6 whose final policies open most solutions with an informal draft in plain text are the 6 best on AIME24 (30.4–34.6%, every other run at most 24.4%). They come from 5 conditions, 2 of them rewarded by the verifier alone. We found this after the runs had finished and defined the measure after reading the completions; the verdicts we logged do not show what set the switch off.

Repetition. Kev-4B pays for solutions that solve the problem again after giving the answer. 6 of the 11 runs with its score in the reward ended repeating themselves, against 2 of the 15 others, and repeating did not make a policy more accurate.

Seeds. The seeds of one condition often differ more than the conditions do. The intervals in these papers resample problems and do not include this variation, so the papers give every seed's result.

Kev-4B 是在 Qwen3.5-4B-Base 上以 LoRA 适配器加指针输出头构建的决策模型，一次前向传播即可回答关于 JSON 状态的是/否问题。每个部分都检验这种判断能否作为有用的训练信号，并在适用时与同等规模的自回归评判模型比较。

  • 第一部分：MuJoCo 上以语言评判为 PPO 奖励。Kev-4B 带门控的奖励的归一化得分四分位均值为 1.00，工程化奖励为 1.05，自回归评判模型为 0.35；在 Reacher 上所有评判奖励最终都不如随机策略。论文
  • 第二部分：策略与评判共用一个骨干，在 MATH 上的 GRPO。共用骨干让训练器加载后少用 48% 的显存；只用验证器就把 MATH-500 从 76.5% 提高到 82.5%，加入 Kev-4B 的条件各有一个种子遥遥领先、其余相近，增益无法归于评判模型。论文
  • 第三部分 A：可见测试、留出测试与评判模型：多轮代码修复中的奖励投机。没有看到可见测试被钻空子的迹象；评判因子带来的变化没有超出种子之间的差异。论文
  • 第三部分 B：验证器只标注部分样本时，评判输出头能否补全标签。冻结的输出头排序良好，但它的策略学会了重复自己，相对于丢掉未标注样本最终为 −2.3 个百分点；从头训练的输出头为 +4.1，两个种子都改成了先写草稿。论文
  • 第三部分 C：评判模型能否为树状 rollout 选择分支前缀。Kev-4B 选根让正确叶子的比例比保留全部四个根时变化 +1.4 个百分点，但最终策略与随机选根一样准确；树状 run 相对扁平 GRPO 的领先主要来自改成先写草稿的种子。论文

三篇 GRPO 论文的共同发现。26 个 GRPO run 中，最终策略的多数解答以纯文本非正式草稿开头的 6 个，正是 AIME24 上最好的 6 个，分属 5 个条件，其中 2 个的奖励只有验证器；这是在 run 结束后才发现的。Kev-4B 会为答完之后把题目再解一遍的解答付分：奖励中含有它的 11 个 run 中有 6 个最终重复自己，其他 15 个中只有 2 个。同一条件的种子之间的差异常常大于条件之间的差异。
