\section{Discussion}\label{sec:discussion}

\paragraph{What the experiment answers.} The question was whether a 4B judge that reads a text summary of the robot's
behaviour can give PPO a reward to learn from. With \kev{}'s gated or distilled reward, PPO learned to move forward on
all four locomotion tasks. On Hopper the final returns are close to those of the engineered reward, and on HalfCheetah
they are higher. On Swimmer they are higher on average but spread widely, and on Walker2d they are lower. On Reacher
every judge's reward failed. The autoregressive judge of the same size and family read the same text and answered the same questions, but its
reward taught the hopper and the walker to stand.

\paragraph{Correlation is not enough.} On Hopper and Walker2d the two gated judges rank windows by the reward almost
equally well, offline (\S\ref{sec:agreement}) and during training (Table~\ref{tab:cost}). Yet one reward trained hopping
and walking, and the other trained standing. The outcome was decided by how much more the judge pays for the behaviour the
task wants than for safe but useless behaviour (Table~\ref{tab:slowfast}). An offline test of a judge as a reward should
report this margin as well as the correlation. Here a fall ends the episode and every step earns a positive reward, so a
small margin favours survival over progress. A judge whose scores crowd at the top of its scale makes the margin small.

\paragraph{A judge's reward follows the task text.} It is not a noisy copy of the engineered reward. The HalfCheetah text
calls flipping undesirable, and no judge's reward let the cheetah flip, while the engineered reward did in
\NUM{flipruns_cheetah_env} of five runs. The Reacher text asks for little control effort, and the summary reports the
effort, yet the arms trained on the judges' rewards flail. Both judges follow how the distance changes within a window more than how large it
is, and with two windows per episode PPO did not find the target. A judge can add a preference that the engineered
reward lacks, and it can miss a cost that the engineered reward charges.

\paragraph{Distillation.} A reward network trained on \kev{}'s labels for a tenth of the windows did as well as asking
\kev{} about every window. In our setup this saving did not shorten the runs, because the judge server was shared. We
expect it to matter when a judge serves a single run, but we did not measure that.

\paragraph{Limitations.} The judges read summaries that we designed from privileged simulator state. The summaries name
the quantities that matter and state a target speed. This favours the judges, and the experiment says nothing about
judging from pixels or raw observations. We used one PPO configuration (CleanRL's defaults) for every condition,
$10^6$ steps, and five seeds (three for \texttt{ar\_sampled}). The intervals are wide, and per-task probabilities of
improvement between about 0.3 and 0.7 should not be read as differences. The AR judge had one prompt format and one
readout (the expectation over the option tokens, renormalised), with no calibration and no reasoning. A different prompt,
a calibrated or rescaled readout, or reasoning before the answer could change its margin. We did not try them, and we did
not tune either judge's prompts. The comparison involves two judges of one family and size. It does not show that
decision models in general make better rewards than generative judges. The costs were measured with one judge server
shared by up to 24 runs. Finally, \NUM{resumed_runs} runs were resumed after a reset of the machine, and each lost one
episode.

\paragraph{Relation to the earlier versions.} Versions 1--3 of this paper reported a leaderboard and a ``100\% win rate
over the human oracle'' for \kev{}'s reward. Those results were not produced by experiments (see the notice at the top and
\file{CORRECTION.md}). The measured answer is narrower. With its gate, or distilled, \kev{}'s reward comes close to the
engineered reward on locomotion. It helps where the task text carries a preference that the engineered reward lacks, and
it fails on Reacher. It is far more usable for PPO than the reward of an autoregressive judge of the same size.
