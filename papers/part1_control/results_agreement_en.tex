\section{Do the judges' scores track the reward?}\label{sec:agreement}

Both judges scored the windows logged by the \texttt{env} runs: \NUM{windows_hopper} for Hopper, \NUM{windows_walker}
for Walker2d, \NUM{windows_cheetah} for HalfCheetah, \NUM{windows_swimmer} for Swimmer and \NUM{windows_reacher} for
Reacher, from all five runs and all of training. Table~\ref{tab:agreement} gives each signal's Spearman correlation with
the window's per-step environment reward. Intervals are 95\% intervals from a two-stage bootstrap that resamples the
runs and then the windows of each run, so the windows of one run are not counted as independent.

\paragraph{\kev{} tracks the reward more closely than the AR judge.} On every task, \kev{}'s \texttt{progress}
correlates more strongly with the environment's reward than the AR judge's expected \texttt{progress}. The difference
is smallest on Walker2d (\NUM{rhod_walker_kevprogress_arprogress}
\ci{\NUM{rhodlo_walker_kevprogress_arprogress}}{\NUM{rhodhi_walker_kevprogress_arprogress}}) and largest on
HalfCheetah (\NUM{rhod_cheetah_kevprogress_arprogress}
\ci{\NUM{rhodlo_cheetah_kevprogress_arprogress}}{\NUM{rhodhi_cheetah_kevprogress_arprogress}}) and Reacher
(\NUM{rhod_reacher_kevprogress_arprogress}
\ci{\NUM{rhodlo_reacher_kevprogress_arprogress}}{\NUM{rhodhi_reacher_kevprogress_arprogress}}). No interval includes
zero. The gated signals keep the order on every task (\NUM{rhod_walker_kevgated_argated} on Walker2d to
\NUM{rhod_reacher_kevgated_argated} on Reacher).

\paragraph{Reacher is the exception that qualifies this.} Reacher's per-step reward is mostly the distance between the
fingertip and the target, a state. The \texttt{progress} levels describe approaching the target and holding it. Against
the reduction of the distance over the window, the quantity the question is mostly about, the two judges are close, with
the AR judge slightly ahead (\kev{} \NUM{rhophys_reacher_kev_progress}, AR \NUM{rhophys_reacher_ar_progress}; difference
\NUM{rhophysd_reacher_kevprogress_arprogress}
\ci{\NUM{rhophysdlo_reacher_kevprogress_arprogress}}{\NUM{rhophysdhi_reacher_kevprogress_arprogress}}). \kev{}'s
higher correlation with Reacher's reward is therefore not a better reading of the approach the question asks about.
On the four locomotion tasks the reward and the forward velocity rank the windows almost identically, and the two
correlations nearly coincide.

\paragraph{Sampling the AR judge's answer discards most of the signal.} A level drawn from the AR judge's distribution
correlates only \NUM{rho_cheetah_ar_progress_sampled} (HalfCheetah) to \NUM{rho_swimmer_ar_progress_sampled} (Swimmer)
with the reward, against \NUM{rho_reacher_ar_progress} to \NUM{rho_walker_ar_progress} for the expected level of the
same distribution. At the scale of one window, the information is in the distribution, and a single draw from it is
mostly noise.

\paragraph{Falls are seen; the gate is mixed.} $p(\text{healthy})$ separates the windows without a fall from those with
one almost perfectly: AUROC \NUM{healthyauc_hopper_kev} and \NUM{healthyauc_walker_kev} for \kev{} on Hopper and
Walker2d, \NUM{healthyauc_hopper_ar} and \NUM{healthyauc_walker_ar} for the AR judge. The summary states whether the
robot fell, so this checks reading rather than judgment. Multiplying \kev{}'s \texttt{progress} by its
$p(\text{healthy})$ lowers the agreement with the reward on four tasks and raises it only on Swimmer
(\NUM{rho_swimmer_kev_gated} against \NUM{rho_swimmer_kev_progress}). For the AR judge the gate also helps on
HalfCheetah (\NUM{rho_cheetah_ar_gated} against \NUM{rho_cheetah_ar_progress}).

\paragraph{The AR judge puts mass outside the answer options.} Before renormalisation, the AR judge's next-token
distribution puts only \NUM{validmass_min_progress} to \NUM{validmass_max_progress} of its mass on the five
\texttt{progress} options, depending on the task, and at least \NUM{validmass_min_healthy} on yes or no. \kev{} has no
such loss, since its head scores only the options.

\paragraph{What this shows and what it does not.} The windows come from policies that optimise the true reward. They
show how well each judge ranks the behaviour such policies produce. They do not show how a judge ranks the behaviour of
a policy that optimises the judge itself; the training runs measure that (\S\ref{sec:results}).
