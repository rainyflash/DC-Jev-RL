% Part II, abstract (included by main_en.tex).
% DRAFT, written with 8 of the 12 GRPO runs; to be checked against the final numbers.
\kev{} is a judge built as a LoRA adapter and a pointer head on Qwen3.5-4B-Base, so a GRPO policy trained as another
LoRA adapter on the same base can share one copy of the backbone with it. We measure what that saves and what the judge
adds to GRPO on MATH. On the shared backbone the judge's probabilities stay within \NUM{shared_parity_max} of its fp32
reference, and the trainer needs \NUM{mem_saving_load} less GPU memory after loading than with the judge loaded
separately. Offline, on \NUM{js_completions} solutions of the base policy, \kev{} tells correct from incorrect about as
well as an autoregressive judge of the same size (AUROC \NUM{js_auc_all_kevc} against \NUM{js_auc_all_arc}); the same
model reasoning before it answers is better, at about \NUM{ratio_gen_kevbb} times the cost. In GRPO (200 steps, 2--3
seeds per condition), the verifier alone raises MATH-500 accuracy from \NUM{acc_base_math}\% to \NUM{acc_rlvr_math}\%.
Adding either judge's probability of correctness to the verifier's reward, or gating the verifier with \kev{}'s score,
changes the final accuracy on MATH-500, GSM8K, AMC23, AIME24 and AIME25 by no more than the evaluation noise. Trained on
\kev{}'s score alone, the policy learns to write solutions that \kev{} rates as correct when they are not. Its
MATH-500 accuracy ends at \NUM{acc_kevonly_math}\%, a difference of \NUM{d_kevonly_math} points to the verifier-trained
policy. Code, logs and every number in this paper are in the repository.
