\section{Does the judge add anything to the verifier?}\label{sec:res-grpo}

\subsection{Learning rate}\label{sec:res-sweep}

The learning rate was chosen once, on the validation set, with the \texttt{rlvr} reward and seed 1. Each sweep run had
60 steps of 16 problems with 8 completions each, at $10^{-5}$, $3\cdot10^{-5}$ or $10^{-4}$ (Table~\ref{tab:sweep}).
The rule takes the highest mean validation accuracy over steps 30 and 60, or the smaller learning rate when the best two
are within 0.5 points (\file{experiments/part2_llm/LR_SELECTION.md}). It was written down while the $10^{-5}$ and
$10^{-4}$ runs were starting. By then the $3\cdot10^{-5}$ run had logged its accuracies at steps 0 and 30, and we had seen
them. $10^{-5}$ (\NUM{sweep_1em5_score}) and $3\cdot10^{-5}$ (\NUM{sweep_3em5_score}) are within 0.5 points, so the rule
chose $10^{-5}$ for every run of Parts II and III. At $10^{-4}$ the validation accuracy rose to \NUM{sweep_1em4_30} by
step 30 and fell to \NUM{sweep_1em4_60} by step 60, below the base model. All three runs start from the same base model,
yet their step-0 accuracies differ (\NUM{sweep_1em5_0}, \NUM{sweep_3em5_0}, \NUM{sweep_1em4_0}). Greedy decoding on vLLM
is not bitwise reproducible across server states, and this spread is the size of the evaluation noise.
