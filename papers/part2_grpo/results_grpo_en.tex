\section{Does the judge add anything to the verifier?}\label{sec:res-grpo}

\subsection{Learning rate}\label{sec:res-sweep}

The learning rate was chosen once, on the validation set, with the \texttt{rlvr} reward and seed 1. Each sweep run had
60 steps of 16 problems with 8 completions each, at $10^{-5}$, $3\cdot10^{-5}$ or $10^{-4}$ (Table~\ref{tab:sweep}).
The rule takes the highest mean validation accuracy over steps 30 and 60, or the smaller learning rate when the best two
are within 0.5 points (\file{experiments/part2_llm/LR_SELECTION.md}). It was written down while the $10^{-5}$ and
$10^{-4}$ runs were starting. By then the $3\cdot10^{-5}$ run had logged its accuracies at steps 0 and 30, and we had seen
them. $10^{-5}$ (\NUM{sweep_1em5_score}) and $3\cdot10^{-5}$ (\NUM{sweep_3em5_score}) are within 0.5 points, so the rule
chose $10^{-5}$ for every run of Parts II and III. At $10^{-4}$ the validation accuracy rose to \NUM{sweep_1em4_30} by
step 30 and fell to \NUM{sweep_1em4_60} by step 60, below the base model. All three runs start from the same base model,
yet their step-0 accuracies differ (\NUM{sweep_1em5_0}, \NUM{sweep_3em5_0}, \NUM{sweep_1em4_0}). Greedy decoding on vLLM
is not bitwise reproducible across server states, and this spread is the size of the evaluation noise.

usable signal for a policy that is not optimised against it (\S\ref{sec:res-study}); it is not a usable reward on its

\subsection{Three ways of writing a solution}\label{sec:res-style}

Figure~\ref{fig:style-example} shows one MATH-500 problem as the final policies of three runs answer it. Most policies
write one formatted solution, with LaTeX and markdown, and end with the boxed answer (left). The two far seeds first
work the problem in plain text, without LaTeX or markdown, give the answer, and then write the formatted solution
(middle). Some policies write the formatted solution, give the answer, and write it again in nearly the same words
(right). We measure the first style by the share of completions that open with an informal draft: no math delimiter
(\texttt{\$}, \texttt{\textbackslash(} or \texttt{\textbackslash[}) and no markdown heading or bold (\texttt{\#\#\#} or
\texttt{**}) in the first 300 characters. We measure the third by the share of a completion's lines (of at least 20
characters) that repeat an earlier line of it. Both measures were defined after we had read the final completions, to
describe what we saw.

\caption{One MATH-500 problem (\texttt{test/prealgebra/307}), the final greedy completion of three Part II runs,
verbatim (runs of blank lines shortened). Left: \texttt{rlvr} seed 1 (\NUM{exmtok_rlvr_s1} tokens). Middle:
\texttt{rlvr\_kev\_gate} seed 2, which drafts first (\NUM{exmtok_kevgate_s2} tokens). Right: \texttt{rlvr} seed 3,
which repeats itself (\NUM{exmtok_rlvr_s3} tokens). All three are right. We chose this problem for illustration, as
one with short completions in plain ASCII that all three runs solve.}
\label{fig:style-example}
\end{figure}

\paragraph{The two far seeds switched to drafting first.} The base model drafts first in \NUM{pdraftat_0_min} to
\NUM{pdraftat_0_max} of its MATH-500 completions at step 0 (Table~\ref{tab:grpo-drafts}). Every run cut this share in
its first 50 steps, to at most \NUM{pdraftat_50_max} at step 50, and in ten of the twelve runs it stayed low (at most
\NUM{pdraftmax_other} in the final evaluation). In \texttt{rlvr\_kev\_gate} seed 2 it rose from \NUM{draftat_kevgate_s2_100} at
step 100 to \NUM{draftat_kevgate_s2_150} at step 150, and in \texttt{rlvr\_kev\_add} seed 3 from
\NUM{draftat_kevadd_s3_150} at step 150 to \NUM{draftat_kevadd_s3_200} at step 200. The final MATH-500 completions of
these two runs open with a draft on \NUM{sdraft_kevgate_s2} and \NUM{sdraft_kevadd_s3} of the problems. They are no
longer than those of the other runs (\NUM{stok_kevgate_s2} and \NUM{stok_kevadd_s3} tokens on average;
Table~\ref{tab:grpo-style}). Runs with the same seed see the same problems in the same order and start from the same
samples, so the switch is not a property of the data order: \texttt{rlvr} seeds 2 and 3 did not switch.

\paragraph{The same switch happened in the other papers, and only in the best runs.} Four runs of Parts III-B and
III-C of this project switched in the same way, among them a run whose reward is the verifier's alone (\texttt{tree\_random}
seed 2 of Part III-C, which differs from \texttt{rlvr} only in how it samples the completions of a group).
Table~\ref{tab:modes-all} lists all \NUM{allruns} GRPO runs of the project by the style of their final policy. The
\NUM{alldraftruns} runs that switched reach \NUM{alldraftaime24min} to \NUM{alldraftaime24max}\% on AIME24, every other
run at most \NUM{allotheraime24max}\%; on MATH-500 they reach \NUM{alldraftmathmin} to \NUM{alldraftmathmax}\%, every
other run at most \NUM{allothermathmax}\%. The classes do not depend on the details of the definition: with an opening
window of 150 or 600 characters instead of 300, or with the math delimiters as the only markers, the runs that switched
still open at least \NUM{alldraftvarmin_draft} of their final completions with a draft and every other run at most
\NUM{alldraftvarmax_other}. We found the switch after the runs had finished, and the classification is ours, so this is
a description of the runs and not a test of a hypothesis stated in advance.

\paragraph{The verdicts do not show why.} GRPO raises the probability of completions with a positive advantage, so a
style spreads when its completions are better than the other completions of their group. Table~\ref{tab:grpo-draftgroups}
compares, within each group of 8 training completions that holds both kinds, how often the drafts and the others are
right. In steps 0--49 drafts were less often right in all \NUM{pdgn_0} runs: the difference was between
\NUM{pdgverdict_0_min} and \NUM{pdgverdict_0_max} points, which is why their share fell. From step 50 on it was between
\NUM{pdglate_draft_min} and \NUM{pdglate_draft_max} points in the two runs that switched, and between
\NUM{pdglate_other_min} and \NUM{pdglate_other_max} in the others, whose groups rarely held a draft. In
\texttt{rlvr\_kev\_add} seed 3, drafts were less often right than the other completions of their groups in every window,
by \NUM{dgverdict_kevadd_s3_150} points over steps 150--199, while their share of the training completions grew from
\NUM{dgshare_kevadd_s3_100} to \NUM{dgshare_kevadd_s3_150}. Their advantage, which includes the judge's term, was not
larger either (\NUM{dgadv_kevadd_s3_150} on average). What made these runs switch, the verdicts and rewards we logged
do not show.

\subsection{Does the judge pay for repetition?}\label{sec:res-repeat}

After the GRPO runs we tested whether the judges pay for repetition (\file{dcjev/llm/repeat_probe.py};
Table~\ref{tab:repeat-probe}). No edit changes the last boxed answer, the one the verifier reads, and each judge scores
the texts as in training.

\paragraph{Repetition as such is worth little to \kev{}.} Writing the last two paragraphs of the base policy's solutions
from the judge study twice more raises \kev{}'s $\pc$ by \NUM{rpkevc_end3_1}
\ci{\NUM{rpkevclo_end3_1}}{\NUM{rpkevchi_end3_1}} on the \NUM{rpn_end3_1} solutions the verifier accepts and changes it
by \NUM{rpkevc_end3_0} \ci{\NUM{rpkevclo_end3_0}}{\NUM{rpkevchi_end3_0}} on the \NUM{rpn_end3_0} it rejects. Writing the
whole solution twice gives \NUM{rpkevc_twice_1} and \NUM{rpkevc_twice_0}. The AR judge moves more, by
\NUM{rparc_end3_1} on right answers and \NUM{rparc_end3_0} on wrong ones.

\paragraph{The repetition the policies learned is paid for.} We took the final MATH-500 solutions of the repeating runs
\texttt{kev\_only} and \texttt{rlvr\_kev\_gate} (seed 1 of each) and \texttt{rlvr} (seed 3) that repeat lines, and cut
each one after its first answer, as if the policy had stopped there. Solutions that repeat before their first answer,
or whose first answer differs from their last, were left out (\NUM{rpskip_kevonly}, \NUM{rpskip_kevgate} and
\NUM{rpskip_rlvr}). \kev{} rates the solutions as written higher than the cut ones. For \texttt{kev\_only}, whose reward
is \kev{}'s $\pc$, the part after the first answer raises it by \NUM{rpkevc_cutkevonly_1}
\ci{\NUM{rpkevclo_cutkevonly_1}}{\NUM{rpkevchi_cutkevonly_1}} on right answers and by \NUM{rpkevc_cutkevonly_0}
\ci{\NUM{rpkevclo_cutkevonly_0}}{\NUM{rpkevchi_cutkevonly_0}} on wrong ones, for \NUM{rpkevcup_cutkevonly_0} of the wrong
ones: from \NUM{rpkevcwithout_cutkevonly_0} to \NUM{rpkevcwith_cutkevonly_0}. \texttt{rlvr\_kev\_gate} multiplies the
verifier's reward by \kev{}'s $\pg$, so among the completions the verifier accepts this factor alone decides which are
paid more. On those, the part after the first answer raises $\pg$ by \NUM{rpkevg_cutkevgate_1}
\ci{\NUM{rpkevglo_cutkevgate_1}}{\NUM{rpkevghi_cutkevgate_1}}, for \NUM{rpkevgup_cutkevgate_1} of them: the gate pays
right answers that repeat themselves more than right answers that stop. \kev{} was not in the reward of \texttt{rlvr}
seed 3, yet it also rates that run's repeating solutions higher as written: $\pc$ by
\NUM{rpkevc_cutrlvr_1} \ci{\NUM{rpkevclo_cutrlvr_1}}{\NUM{rpkevchi_cutrlvr_1}} on right answers and
\NUM{rpkevc_cutrlvr_0} \ci{\NUM{rpkevclo_cutrlvr_0}}{\NUM{rpkevchi_cutrlvr_0}} on wrong ones, $\pg$ by
\NUM{rpkevg_cutrlvr_1} on right ones. The AR judge, in none of these rewards, also rates these solutions higher with the
part after the first answer (by \NUM{rparc_cutkevonly_1}, \NUM{rparc_cutkevgate_1} and \NUM{rparc_cutrlvr_1} on right
answers), though less consistently.

The repetition we added is a verbatim copy, while the policies solve the problem again in slightly different words.
Which property of their repetition the judge pays for, the probe does not tell. It does show that what these policies
learned to write after their first answer raises the judge's score, so a reward with the judge in it pays for it, and
it does so whether or not the policy was trained against the judge.
