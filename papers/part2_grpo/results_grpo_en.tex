\section{Does the judge add anything to the verifier?}\label{sec:res-grpo}

\subsection{Learning rate}\label{sec:res-sweep}

The learning rate was chosen once, on the validation set, with the \texttt{rlvr} reward and seed 1. Each sweep run had
60 steps of 16 problems with 8 completions each, at $10^{-5}$, $3\cdot10^{-5}$ or $10^{-4}$ (Table~\ref{tab:sweep}).
The rule takes the highest mean validation accuracy over steps 30 and 60, or the smaller learning rate when the best two
are within 0.5 points (\file{experiments/part2_llm/LR_SELECTION.md}). It was written down while the $10^{-5}$ and
$10^{-4}$ runs were starting. By then the $3\cdot10^{-5}$ run had logged its accuracies at steps 0 and 30, and we had seen
them. $10^{-5}$ (\NUM{sweep_1em5_score}) and $3\cdot10^{-5}$ (\NUM{sweep_3em5_score}) are within 0.5 points, so the rule
chose $10^{-5}$ for every run of Parts II and III. At $10^{-4}$ the validation accuracy rose to \NUM{sweep_1em4_30} by
step 30 and fell to \NUM{sweep_1em4_60} by step 60, below the base model. All three runs start from the same base model,
yet their step-0 accuracies differ (\NUM{sweep_1em5_0}, \NUM{sweep_3em5_0}, \NUM{sweep_1em4_0}). Greedy decoding on vLLM
is not bitwise reproducible across server states, and this spread is the size of the evaluation noise.

\paragraph{Adding a judge to the verifier changes nothing we can measure.} With \kev{}'s $\pc$ added to the verifier's
reward, the final policy differs from \texttt{rlvr} by \NUM{d_kevadd_math} \ci{\NUM{dlo_kevadd_math}}{\NUM{dhi_kevadd_math}}
points on MATH-500 and \NUM{d_kevadd_gsm} \ci{\NUM{dlo_kevadd_gsm}}{\NUM{dhi_kevadd_gsm}} on GSM8K. With the AR judge's
$P_{\mathrm{a}}$ the differences are \NUM{d_aradd_math} \ci{\NUM{dlo_aradd_math}}{\NUM{dhi_aradd_math}} and
\NUM{d_aradd_gsm} \ci{\NUM{dlo_aradd_gsm}}{\NUM{dhi_aradd_gsm}}. On the competition sets the intervals are a few points
wide either way. Gating the verifier by \kev{}'s \emph{genuine} score (\texttt{rlvr\_kev\_gate}) does not change the
accuracy either (\NUM{d_kevgate_math} \ci{\NUM{dlo_kevgate_math}}{\NUM{dhi_kevgate_math}} on MATH-500), but it changes
what the policy writes. Its MATH-500 solutions are \NUM{stok_kevgate} tokens long on average against \NUM{stok_rlvr}
for \texttt{rlvr}, and \NUM{srep_kevgate} of their lines repeat an earlier line of the same solution, against
\NUM{srep_rlvr} (Table~\ref{tab:grpo-style}). Among completions with the same verdict, the judge term decides which is
better, as designed, and under it the policy learns to repeat itself without becoming more accurate; whether the
judge's score itself rises with repetition is tested in \S\ref{sec:res-repeat}. % TODO: rewrite with the probe's result.

\paragraph{The judge alone is exploited.} Without the verifier (\texttt{kev\_only}), the policy maximises \kev{}'s
$\pc$, which rises to \NUM{kevc_kevonly} on the training completions over the last 20 steps (\texttt{rlvr}:
\NUM{kevc_rlvr}). The verifier's accuracy on the same completions stays at \NUM{trainacc_kevonly}\%
(\texttt{rlvr}: \NUM{trainacc_rlvr}\%). Against \texttt{rlvr}, the final policy's accuracy differs by
\NUM{d_kevonly_math} \ci{\NUM{dlo_kevonly_math}}{\NUM{dhi_kevonly_math}} points on MATH-500 and by
\NUM{d_kevonly_aime24} on AIME24, and it ends below the base model it started from on MATH-500 (\NUM{acc_kevonly_math}\% against
\NUM{acc_base_math}\%) and on all three competition sets. What it learned is to write solutions that \kev{} rates
as correct: \kev{} gives its wrong MATH-500 answers \NUM{skevwrong_kevonly} on average, against \NUM{skevwrong_rlvr} for
the wrong answers of \texttt{rlvr}'s policy. The within-group AUROC of \kev{} on the training completions, which is
what GRPO's advantage uses, falls from \NUM{kevaucwtr_first20_kevonly} in the first 20 steps to
\NUM{kevaucwtr_last20_kevonly} in the last 20. Its solutions repeat themselves too: \NUM{srep_kevonly} of their lines
repeat an earlier one. A 4B judge is a usable signal for a policy that is not optimised against it
(\S\ref{sec:res-study}); it is not a usable reward on its own.

\paragraph{Evaluation noise.} Every run evaluates the base model at step 0. Across the \NUM{szruns_math500} runs, MATH-500
accuracy at step 0 ranges from \NUM{szmin_math500}\% to \NUM{szmax_math500}\% (standard deviation
\NUM{szsd_math500} points) and validation accuracy from \NUM{szmin_val}\% to \NUM{szmax_val}\%. The final weights of
each run are evaluated twice on MATH-500, both times greedily: at step 200 with the periodic evaluations, and in the
final evaluation. The two results differ by \NUM{rptabs_math500} points on average and by up to \NUM{rptmax_math500}
(\NUM{rptruns_math500} runs). Differences of a point between two final policies are within this noise.
