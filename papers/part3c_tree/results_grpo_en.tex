\section{Training with tree rollouts}\label{sec:res-tree}

Both tree conditions ran with \NUM{seeds_treekev} seeds of 200 steps, everything else as in Part II's \texttt{rlvr},
which is the flat reference (\NUM{seeds_rlvr} seeds). Runs with the same seed see the same problems in the same order.
We first ran two seeds of each tree condition. Each condition then had one seed far above \texttt{rlvr} and one at its
level, which left the effect of the tree shape undecided, so after both had finished we added a third seed of each
(\file{scripts/make_jobs.sh}). That decision was made after seeing the first results; the third seeds are reported like
the others. Table~\ref{tab:tree-acc} gives the final accuracies, Table~\ref{tab:tree-seeds} those of each seed,
Table~\ref{tab:tree-paired} the paired differences, Table~\ref{tab:tree-stats} what happened inside the trees during
training, and Figure~\ref{fig:tree-curves} the training curves. The intervals resample problems and do not include the
variation between seeds.

\subsection{The judge's choice of roots}\label{sec:res-tree-choice}

\paragraph{The judge's choice gained little over a random one.} Every tenth step the roots that were not kept are
continued too, so we know what keeping the chosen two gained over keeping all four (Table~\ref{tab:tree-stats}). Under
\texttt{tree\_kev} the leaves of the two roots \kev{} ranked highest were correct \NUM{auditgain_all_treekev}
\ci{\NUM{auditgainlo_all_treekev}}{\NUM{auditgainhi_all_treekev}} points more often than the leaves of all four roots,
over \NUM{auditgain_records_treekev} audited steps. Under \texttt{tree\_random}, whose choice gains nothing in
expectation, the same number is \NUM{auditgain_all_treerand}
\ci{\NUM{auditgainlo_all_treerand}}{\NUM{auditgainhi_all_treerand}}; these intervals resample the audited steps.
Keeping the two roots whose leaves turned out best would have gained \NUM{auditbest_all_treekev} points, more than any
ranking made before the leaves exist could, since the same four leaves per root both rank the roots and score them. On
the audited steps \kev{}'s \emph{on track} score of a root separates its correct from its incorrect leaves with an AUROC
of \NUM{auditotauc_all_treekev} pooled over prompts, but only \NUM{auditotaucw_all_treekev}
\ci{\NUM{auditotaucwlo_all_treekev}}{\NUM{auditotaucwhi_all_treekev}} within a prompt, the comparison that decides which
roots are kept (\NUM{auditotaucw_all_treerand} under \texttt{tree\_random}). Offline, with the same structure (four
prefixes of 128 tokens per problem, four continuations each), the within-problem AUROC was \NUM{aucw_128_kevtrack}, and
keeping the two best-ranked prefixes raised the share of continuations ending correct by \NUM{gainpt_128_kevtrack}
points (\S\ref{sec:res-prefix}). The prefixes differ: offline they are the base policy's, in training the policy's own
as it learns. Its roots lead to correct answers more and more often (under \texttt{tree\_random},
\NUM{auditval_firsthalf_treerand}\% of the audited leaves were correct in the first half of training and
\NUM{auditval_secondhalf_treerand}\% in the second, against \NUM{ps_valueallpct_128}\% of the continuations of the base
policy's prefixes offline), and the room for a choice, the best two in hindsight, shrank from \NUM{auditbest_firsthalf_treerand}
to \NUM{auditbest_secondhalf_treerand} points.

\paragraph{Final accuracy: no effect of the judge's choice.} With the same tree shape, keeping \kev{}'s roots instead
of random ones changed the final accuracy by \NUM{dvtreerand_treekev_math}
\ci{\NUM{dvtreerandlo_treekev_math}}{\NUM{dvtreerandhi_treekev_math}} points on MATH-500, \NUM{dvtreerand_treekev_gsm}
\ci{\NUM{dvtreerandlo_treekev_gsm}}{\NUM{dvtreerandhi_treekev_gsm}} on GSM8K, \NUM{dvtreerand_treekev_amc}
\ci{\NUM{dvtreerandlo_treekev_amc}}{\NUM{dvtreerandhi_treekev_amc}} on AMC23, \NUM{dvtreerand_treekev_aime24}
\ci{\NUM{dvtreerandlo_treekev_aime24}}{\NUM{dvtreerandhi_treekev_aime24}} on AIME24 and \NUM{dvtreerand_treekev_aime25}
\ci{\NUM{dvtreerandlo_treekev_aime25}}{\NUM{dvtreerandhi_treekev_aime25}} on AIME25 (Table~\ref{tab:tree-paired}). No
interval excludes zero; the one on GSM8K just reaches it.

\paragraph{Against flat sampling, the seed means follow the seeds that switched.} In the seed mean both tree conditions
end above \texttt{rlvr}, by \NUM{d_treerand_math} and \NUM{d_treekev_math} points on MATH-500 and by
\NUM{d_treerand_aime24} and \NUM{d_treekev_aime24} on AIME24. Seed by seed the picture is different
(Table~\ref{tab:tree-seeds}). \NUM{treedraftruns} of the tree runs switched to opening their solutions in plain text
(\S\ref{sec:res-tree-drafts}), and they end at \NUM{treedraftmathmin}--\NUM{treedraftmathmax}\% on MATH-500 and
\NUM{treedraftaime24min}--\NUM{treedraftaime24max}\% on AIME24. The other \NUM{treeotherruns} end at
\NUM{treeothermathmin}--\NUM{treeothermathmax}\% and \NUM{treeotheraime24min}--\NUM{treeotheraime24max}\%, and the
\NUM{flatruns} seeds of \texttt{rlvr}, none of which switched, at \NUM{flatmathmin}--\NUM{flatmathmax}\% and
\NUM{flataime24min}--\NUM{flataime24max}\%. The seed means of a condition thus mostly count how many of its seeds
switched. Whether the tree shape helps a policy that does not switch, or makes the switch more likely, these runs
cannot tell. At step 100, when no run drafted in more than \NUM{pdraftat_100_max} of its MATH-500 completions, neither
tree condition was ahead on MATH-500 (\NUM{st100acc_treerand_math}\% and \NUM{st100acc_treekev_math}\%, against
\NUM{st100acc_rlvr_math}\% for \texttt{rlvr}).

\subsection{Opening in plain text}\label{sec:res-tree-drafts}

Part II found that some GRPO runs switch, abruptly, to opening their completions with an informal draft in plain text,
without LaTeX or markdown, and that the runs that switch are the best ones (Part II, \S8.3). We measure the style as
there, by the share of completions that open with such a draft: no math delimiter, markdown heading or bold in the
first 300 characters, a measure defined after reading the completions.

\paragraph{One seed of each tree condition switched.} The base model opens \NUM{pdraftat_0_min} to
\NUM{pdraftat_0_max} of its MATH-500 completions this way, and every run cut that share in its first 50 steps, to at
most \NUM{pdraftat_50_max} at step 50 (Table~\ref{tab:tree-drafts}). In \texttt{tree\_random} seed 2 it then rose from
\NUM{draftat_treerand_s2_100} at step 100 to \NUM{draftat_treerand_s2_150} at step 150, and in \texttt{tree\_kev} seed 1
from \NUM{draftat_treekev_s1_150} at step 150 to \NUM{draftat_treekev_s1_200} at step 200. Every other run, the \NUM{flatruns} of
\texttt{rlvr} included, opens at most \NUM{pdraftmax_other} of its final completions this way. The two runs that switched
write differently from those of Part II: in most of their completions the plain text is the whole solution, and only
\NUM{sdraftfmt_treerand_s2} and \NUM{sdraftfmt_treekev_s1} of their drafts go on to formatted text, against
\NUM{alldraftfmt_kevgate_s2} and \NUM{alldraftfmt_kevadd_s3} in the runs of Part II that switched (\texttt{rlvr\_kev\_gate}
seed 2 and \texttt{rlvr\_kev\_add} seed 3).

In a tree, how a completion opens is settled by its root. The four leaves of a root share its first 128 tokens, which
nearly always hold the first 300 characters: they agree on whether they open with a draft for all but at most
\NUM{prootsmixedmax} of the roots of a run. Keeping two roots of four is thus also a choice among openings, made by
\kev{} under \texttt{tree\_kev} and at random under \texttt{tree\_random}; the random choice did not keep a run from
switching.

\paragraph{The verdicts do not show why.} As in Part II, we compare within each group of training completions that

group has one root of each kind. In steps 0--49 drafts were less often right than the others in
all \NUM{pdgn_0} runs (the difference was between \NUM{pdgverdict_0_min} and \NUM{pdgverdict_0_max} points), which is
why their share fell. In the windows in which they spread, they were about as often right as the others:
\NUM{dgverdict_treerand_s2_100} points in \texttt{tree\_random} seed 2 over steps 100--149, when
\NUM{dgshare_treerand_s2_100} of its training completions were drafts, and \NUM{dgverdict_treekev_s1_150} in
\texttt{tree\_kev} seed 1 over steps 150--199 (\NUM{dgshare_treekev_s1_150}). What made these runs switch, the verdicts
we logged do not show. Across the \NUM{allruns} GRPO runs of this project, the \NUM{alldraftruns} that switched, in
\NUM{alldraftconds} conditions and \NUM{allveronlydraft} of them with the verifier's reward alone, are the
\NUM{alldraftruns} best on AIME24 (\NUM{alldraftaime24min} to \NUM{alldraftaime24max}\%, every other run at most
\NUM{allotheraime24max}\%; Part II, \S8.3).

\subsection{Repetition}\label{sec:res-tree-repeat}

\paragraph{One seed of \texttt{tree\_kev} came to repeat itself, as one of \texttt{rlvr} did.} The training completions
of \texttt{tree\_kev}'s seed 2 came to repeat part of the solution after the first answer: \NUM{roansrep_treekev_s2_0}
of them did in steps 0--24, \NUM{roansrep_treekev_s2_50} in steps 50--74, \NUM{roansrep_treekev_s2_100} in steps
100--124, \NUM{roansrep_treekev_s2_150} in steps 150--174 and \NUM{roansrep_treekev_s2_175} in steps 175--199
(Table~\ref{tab:tree-repeat}; the share first exceeded a fifth in the window from step \NUM{roonset_treekev_s2}). They
grew from \NUM{rotok_treekev_s2_0} to \NUM{rotok_treekev_s2_175} tokens on average; in the last 25 steps
\NUM{rotrunc_treekev_s2_175} of them were cut off at the length limit of 1024 tokens and the verifier accepted
\NUM{roright_treekev_s2_175}. The final policy repeats after its first answer on \NUM{sansrep_treekev_s2} of the
MATH-500 problems, writes \NUM{stok_treekev_s2} tokens per solution, and its accuracy, \NUM{acc_treekev_math_s2}\%, is
that of \texttt{rlvr}. Seed 3 of \texttt{rlvr} did the same (from the window at step \NUM{roonset_rlvr_s3}; its final
policy repeats on \NUM{sansrep_rlvr_s3} of the problems). In every other run at most \NUM{roansrepmax_other} of the
training completions of any window from step 50 on repeated after the answer. More than
one boxed answer is not repetition: \texttt{tree\_random} seed 2 gives several on \NUM{sboxmany_treerand_s2} of the
MATH-500 problems while \NUM{srep_treerand_s2} of its lines repeat an earlier one, against \NUM{srep_treekev_s2} for
seed 2 of \texttt{tree\_kev}.

Both runs that repeat are rewarded by the verifier alone, which reads the last answer, so a repetition that restates
the answer costs them nothing. \kev{} would have paid for it, had it scored whole solutions: on the final MATH-500
solutions of \texttt{tree\_kev} seed 2 that repeat lines, its $\pc$ is \NUM{rpkevc_cuttreekev_1}
\ci{\NUM{rpkevclo_cuttreekev_1}}{\NUM{rpkevchi_cuttreekev_1}} higher as written than cut after the first answer on the
\NUM{rpn_cuttreekev_1} the verifier accepts, and \NUM{rpkevc_cuttreekev_0}
\ci{\NUM{rpkevclo_cuttreekev_0}}{\NUM{rpkevchi_cuttreekev_0}} higher on the \NUM{rpn_cuttreekev_0} it rejects (the probe
of Part II, \S8.4). Under \texttt{tree\_kev}, however, it scores only the roots, the first 128 tokens, long before any
answer, so its choice cannot have paid for the repetition, and \texttt{rlvr} seed 3 repeats with no judge at all.

\subsection{Cost and evaluation noise}\label{sec:res-tree-cost}

\paragraph{Cost.} A tree samples four roots of at most 128 tokens per prompt and continues two of them four times each,
so the leaves of a root share its tokens. In the last 20 steps the tree runs generated \NUM{gensavemin_treerand} to
\NUM{gensavemax_treerand} (\texttt{tree\_random}) and \NUM{gensavemin_treekev} to \NUM{gensavemax_treekev}
(\texttt{tree\_kev}) fewer tokens than flat sampling of completions of the same lengths would have. Per step,
\texttt{tree\_random} generated \NUM{gentok_treerand} thousand tokens and \texttt{tree\_kev} \NUM{gentok_treekev}
thousand, against \NUM{gentok_rlvr} thousand for \texttt{rlvr}; these means also depend on how long each seed's
completions grew (\texttt{rlvr} seed 3 and \texttt{tree\_kev} seed 2, which
repeat themselves, generated \NUM{gentok_rlvr_s3} and \NUM{gentok_treekev_s2} thousand). Scoring the roots with \kev{}
on the shared backbone is the only extra cost of the judge's choice.

\paragraph{Evaluation noise.} Across the \NUM{szruns_math500} runs in this comparison, the base model's MATH-500
accuracy at step 0 ranges from \NUM{szmin_math500}\% to \NUM{szmax_math500}\%, and a run's accuracy changes by
\NUM{lecabs_math500} points on average between its evaluations at steps 150 and 200. The final weights of each run are
evaluated twice on MATH-500, both times greedily: at step 200 with the periodic evaluations, and in the final
evaluation. The two results differ by \NUM{rptabs_math500} points on average and by up to \NUM{rptmax_math500}
(\NUM{rptruns_math500} runs). A difference of about a point between two single evaluations is within the noise of the
evaluation itself.
