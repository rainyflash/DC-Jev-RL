\section{Training with tree rollouts}\label{sec:res-tree}

Both tree conditions ran with \NUM{seeds_treekev} seeds of 200 steps, everything else as in Part II's \texttt{rlvr},
which is the flat reference (\NUM{seeds_rlvr} seeds). Table~\ref{tab:tree-acc} gives the final policies' accuracies,
Table~\ref{tab:tree-paired} the paired differences, Table~\ref{tab:tree-stats} what happened inside the trees during
training, and Figure~\ref{fig:tree-curves} the training curves. The intervals resample problems and do not include the
variation between seeds.

\paragraph{The judge's choice gained little over a random one.} Every tenth step the roots that were not kept are
continued too, so we know what keeping the chosen two gained over keeping all four (Table~\ref{tab:tree-stats}). Under
\texttt{tree\_kev} the leaves of the two roots \kev{} ranked highest were correct \NUM{auditgain_all_treekev}
\ci{\NUM{auditgainlo_all_treekev}}{\NUM{auditgainhi_all_treekev}} points more often than the leaves of all four roots,
over \NUM{auditgain_records_treekev} audited steps. Under \texttt{tree\_random}, whose choice gains nothing in
expectation, the same number is \NUM{auditgain_all_treerand}
\ci{\NUM{auditgainlo_all_treerand}}{\NUM{auditgainhi_all_treerand}}; these intervals resample the audited steps.
Keeping the two roots whose leaves turned out best would have gained \NUM{auditbest_all_treekev} points, more than any
ranking made before the leaves exist could, since the same four leaves per root both rank the roots and score them. On
the audited steps \kev{}'s \emph{on track} score of a root separates its correct from its incorrect leaves with an AUROC
of \NUM{auditotauc_all_treekev} pooled over prompts, but only \NUM{auditotaucw_all_treekev}
\ci{\NUM{auditotaucwlo_all_treekev}}{\NUM{auditotaucwhi_all_treekev}} within a prompt, the comparison that decides which
roots are kept (\NUM{auditotaucw_all_treerand} under \texttt{tree\_random}). Offline, with the same structure (four
prefixes of 128 tokens per problem, four continuations each), the within-problem AUROC was \NUM{aucw_128_kevtrack}, and
keeping the two best-ranked prefixes raised the share of continuations ending correct by \NUM{gainpt_128_kevtrack}
points (\S\ref{sec:res-prefix}). The prefixes differ: offline they are the base policy's, in training the policy's own
as it learns. Its roots lead to correct answers more and more often (under \texttt{tree\_random},
\NUM{auditval_firsthalf_treerand}\% of the audited leaves were correct in the first half of training and
\NUM{auditval_secondhalf_treerand}\% in the second, against \NUM{ps_valueallpct_128}\% of the continuations of the base
policy's prefixes offline), and the room for a choice, the best two in hindsight, shrank from \NUM{auditbest_firsthalf_treerand}
to \NUM{auditbest_secondhalf_treerand} points.

\paragraph{Final accuracy: no effect of the judge's choice; the tree shape undecided.} The two tree conditions end at
the same accuracy. \texttt{tree\_kev} minus \texttt{tree\_random} is \NUM{dvtreerand_treekev_math}
\ci{\NUM{dvtreerandlo_treekev_math}}{\NUM{dvtreerandhi_treekev_math}} points on MATH-500,
\NUM{dvtreerand_treekev_amc} \ci{\NUM{dvtreerandlo_treekev_amc}}{\NUM{dvtreerandhi_treekev_amc}} on AMC23 and
\NUM{dvtreerand_treekev_aime24} \ci{\NUM{dvtreerandlo_treekev_aime24}}{\NUM{dvtreerandhi_treekev_aime24}} on AIME24
(Table~\ref{tab:tree-paired}); only the interval on GSM8K, \NUM{dvtreerand_treekev_gsm}
\ci{\NUM{dvtreerandlo_treekev_gsm}}{\NUM{dvtreerandhi_treekev_gsm}}, excludes zero. Both tree conditions end above flat
sampling in the seed mean (\NUM{d_treerand_math} and \NUM{d_treekev_math} points on MATH-500, \NUM{d_treerand_aime24}
and \NUM{d_treekev_aime24} on AIME24, against \texttt{rlvr}), but not seed by seed: each has one seed that ends well
above \texttt{rlvr} and one that ends at its level. On MATH-500 \texttt{tree\_random} ends at
\NUM{acc_treerand_math_s1}\% and \NUM{acc_treerand_math_s2}\%, \texttt{tree\_kev} at \NUM{acc_treekev_math_s1}\% and
\NUM{acc_treekev_math_s2}\%, and \texttt{rlvr} at \NUM{acc_rlvr_math_s1}\% and \NUM{acc_rlvr_math_s2}\%; on AIME24 at
\NUM{acc_treerand_aime24_s1}\% and \NUM{acc_treerand_aime24_s2}\%, \NUM{acc_treekev_aime24_s1}\% and
\NUM{acc_treekev_aime24_s2}\%, and \NUM{acc_rlvr_aime24_s1}\% and \NUM{acc_rlvr_aime24_s2}\%. The intervals over
problems do not include this variation between seeds, so whether the tree shape itself helps, two seeds cannot tell.
At step 100 neither tree condition was ahead on MATH-500 (\NUM{st100acc_treerand_math}\% and
\NUM{st100acc_treekev_math}\%, against \NUM{st100acc_rlvr_math}\% for \texttt{rlvr}).

\paragraph{One seed of \texttt{tree\_kev} began to repeat itself.} The training completions of \texttt{tree\_kev}'s
seed 2 came to repeat part of the solution after the first answer: \NUM{roansrep_treekev_s2_0} of them did in steps
0--24, \NUM{roansrep_treekev_s2_50} in steps 50--74, \NUM{roansrep_treekev_s2_100} in steps 100--124,
\NUM{roansrep_treekev_s2_150} in steps 150--174 and \NUM{roansrep_treekev_s2_175} in steps 175--199
(Table~\ref{tab:tree-repeat}; the share first exceeded a fifth in the window from step \NUM{roonset_treekev_s2}). They
grew from \NUM{rotok_treekev_s2_0} to \NUM{rotok_treekev_s2_175} tokens on average; in the last 25 steps
\NUM{rotrunc_treekev_s2_175} of them were cut off at the length limit of 1024 tokens and the verifier accepted
\NUM{roright_treekev_s2_175}. Seed 1 of \texttt{tree\_kev} and both seeds of \texttt{tree\_random} did not (at most
\NUM{roansrepmax_treekev_s1}, \NUM{roansrepmax_treerand_s1} and \NUM{roansrepmax_treerand_s2} in any window from step
50 on). The final policy of seed 2 repeats after its first answer
on \NUM{sansrep_treekev_s2} of the MATH-500 problems, that of seed 1 on \NUM{sansrep_treekev_s1}; it writes
\NUM{stok_treekev_s2} tokens per solution against \NUM{stok_treekev_s1}, and its accuracy, \NUM{acc_treekev_math_s2}\%,
is that of \texttt{rlvr}. Several boxed answers alone are not repetition: seed 2 of \texttt{tree\_random} gives more
than one on \NUM{sboxmany_treerand_s2} of the MATH-500 problems (seed 1: \NUM{sboxmany_treerand_s1}) without
repeating lines (\NUM{srep_treerand_s2} of its lines repeat an earlier one, against \NUM{srep_treekev_s2} for seed 2 of
\texttt{tree\_kev}; Table~\ref{tab:tree-style}). This is not the
judge paying for repetition, as in Part II: the reward is the verifier's alone, and \kev{} sees only a solution's first
128 tokens. Under the verifier, which reads the last answer, a repetition that restates the answer costs nothing.
Whether the judge's choice of roots made the policy repeat itself, one seed of two cannot tell.

\paragraph{Cost.} A tree samples four roots of at most 128 tokens per prompt and continues two of them four times each,
so for the same completion length it generates fewer tokens than flat sampling: \NUM{gentok_treerand} thousand per
step under \texttt{tree\_random}, against \NUM{gentok_rlvr} thousand for flat sampling (the last 20 steps).
\texttt{tree\_kev} generated \NUM{gentok_treekev} thousand, because the completions of its seed 2 grew long (above).
Scoring the roots with \kev{} on the shared backbone is the only extra cost of the judge's choice.

\paragraph{Evaluation noise.} Across the \NUM{szruns_math500} runs in this comparison, the base model's MATH-500
accuracy at step 0 ranges from \NUM{szmin_math500}\% to \NUM{szmax_math500}\%, and a run's accuracy changes by
\NUM{lecabs_math500} points on average between its evaluations at steps 150 and 200. The final weights of each run are
evaluated twice on MATH-500, both times greedily: at step 200 with the periodic evaluations, and in the final
evaluation. The two results differ by \NUM{rptabs_math500} points on average and by up to \NUM{rptmax_math500}
(\NUM{rptruns_math500} runs). A difference of about a point between two single evaluations is within the noise of the
evaluation itself.
